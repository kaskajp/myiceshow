# Figure skating move catalog — research draft

Research date: **November 8, 2026**. Status: **First pass, not a complete or coach-validated catalog.**

## Purpose and boundaries

This catalog starts the research requested in [the product brief](../BRIEF.md). It identifies movement families, a proposed difficulty progression, and the animation work they imply. It is not skating instruction, an assessment syllabus, or a claim that every documented variation is covered.

The first pass covers singles and marks partner-only elements separately. Ice dance patterns, synchronized formations, adaptive skating, historical elements, and unusual named variations need further research. A movement family is not an animation: each supported variation may require a different asset and transition.

### Assumptions

- Children choreograph a fictional performance; they are not taught to attempt these elements on ice.
- Difficulty describes skating execution, not the child's age or an unlock requirement.
- Entry, edge, direction, rotation count, position, speed, and exit can change difficulty.
- Prerequisites below are **proposed preparation for expert review**, not official test requirements or an executable dependency graph.
- Animation estimates are **product hypotheses**, not measured production costs.

### Completion criteria for the research

- Each launch variation has a precise definition, technical source, and reviewed demonstration of correct execution.
- Difficulty disagreements and prerequisite uncertainties are resolved or explicitly retained as uncertainties.
- A movement animator assesses contact, blade paths, balance, transitions, and outfit deformation.
- The launch selection covers contrasting movement types and energies, with its size justified by that assessment.

## Reading the catalog

Proposed technical tiers: **B** beginner, **I** intermediate, **A** advanced, **E** elite. A range means that execution or variation changes the tier; these labels are not an ISU or federation grading system. Competition levels are not interchangeable with a universal learning order.

Proposed animation complexity:

- **Low:** simple displacement or pose changes, with blade contact still needing review.
- **Medium:** curved travel, changing support feet, or sustained balance.
- **High:** airborne rotation, rapid turns, spin pivots, or difficult positions.
- **Very high:** coupled skaters or complex multi-element transitions.

Sources are linked by identifier in the final column. **All per-variation demonstration reviews are pending.** S4 and S6 provide starting points for demonstration searches; a page or written syllabus is not a reviewed demonstration. A family source does not establish every listed variation. Entries marked **Source pending** still need a primary reference. No animation is approved from this table alone.

## Demonstration and expert review backlog

Every row still needs variation-level review. For each approved variation, record:

1. Demonstration URL, publisher, timestamp or lesson segment, and access date.
2. Exact foot, edge, direction, entry, exit, and rotation/position shown.
3. Whether the clip demonstrates a learning drill or the completed element.
4. Named reviewer and the balance, blade contact, and timing corrections they require.
5. Reference-use permission before copying footage, poses, or choreography into production assets.

Written descriptions support terminology; they do not prove that an animation is correct. A public video is a viewing reference, not a license to reuse it. If no suitable demonstration exists, retain the gap and seek an expert-produced reference.

## Product implications — proposals, not validated requirements

### Choose the collection after an animation spike

Do not set a final move count yet. The source review supports researching a broad solo vocabulary rather than reducing the app to a handful of tricks. Use these candidate groups for cost assessment:

- **Travel and flow:** glides, stroking, crossovers, slalom, edges, and simple steps.
- **Shapes and expression:** dip, lunge, spiral, presentation poses, spread eagle, and Ina Bauer.
- **Turns and rhythm:** two-foot turns, three-turns, C steps, and selected connecting steps.
- **Spins:** two-foot, upright, back upright, sit, and camel.
- **Highlights:** bunny hop, waltz jump, and selected single jumps after expert review.

This is a shortlist of families to evaluate, not an approved launch manifest. Variation count, reference quality, production cost, and visual distinctness determine the eventual collection. Advanced positions, multi-revolution jumps, and partner elements need explicit budget decisions; higher technical difficulty is not automatically better play value.

### Small technical spike, not a small launch collection

Test an untextured skater with a glide, three-turn, upright spin, waltz jump, and lunge. These deliberately exercise travel, support-foot changes, spinning, airborne motion, and a low pose. They are a validation set, not a six-to-eight-move launch cap.

Evaluate candidate rendering approaches against the same checks:

- Blade contact remains plausible during glides, turns, spins, and landings.
- The skater travels and faces correctly without foot sliding or transition teleporting.
- Entry/exit transitions work across the selected moves, not only isolated clips.
- A planned routine plays against a fixed audio clock without visible accumulated drift.
- Duration changes preserve jump flight and landing timing rather than stretching every clip indiscriminately.
- Outfit changes do not break poses, and the camera keeps the performance readable.
- Performance is measured on a selected physical iOS device; the minimum supported device remains undecided.

The [animation lab](animation-prototype.md) provisionally implements this validation set using SwiftUI and RealityKit on iOS 18 or later. This is an experiment, not a production engine, asset-pipeline, or minimum-device decision. Physical-device measurements and production animation comparisons are still pending.

### Information the app will eventually need

A future variation record should retain a stable identifier, family, child-facing label, technical name and aliases, category, proposed tier, source, demonstration-review status, support foot/edge, and applicable rotation or position. Animation metadata separately needs entry/exit state, travel displacement, timing landmarks, permitted duration adjustments, and asset identifiers.

These are research needs, not a committed schema. Keep technical metadata separate from the illustrated cards children use. Do not expose a technical prerequisite graph as mandatory progression unless the product design later calls for that.

## Unresolved questions and coverage gaps

- **Completeness:** 59 family records do not enumerate all variations or disciplines. Historical, unusual, and choreographic movements remain undercovered.
- **Demonstrations:** no per-variation clip has been reviewed. The brief's correct-execution reference requirement is not complete.
- **Difficulty:** the B/I/A/E mapping and proposed preparation need coaching review. Curriculum placement, competition scoring, flexibility, and learning difficulty measure different things.
- **Taxonomy:** distinguish family, position, entry, variation, and full sequence before building filters or generating assets.
- **Direction:** decide whether the app supports both rotational directions and both feet; visual mirroring alone needs expert validation.
- **Source season:** S3 and S5 are listed as 2026/27 handbooks. S2 is older curriculum material, not current competition rules; do not treat these sources as synchronized editions.
- **Source coverage:** F02 and J01 still lack primary references. Several family references do not establish all the listed directional variations; these need individual confirmation.
- **Technology and cost:** the procedural RealityKit lab is not a production rig or an animation-source decision. Production estimates, rendering comparisons, and physical-device benchmarks remain pending.
- **Production rights:** no reference footage, animation, character, or music asset has been licensed by this research.
